# Articles: format guide

## Frontmatter (all articles)

```yaml
title: "Article Title"
date: "YYYY-MM-DD"
sourceName: "Publisher of the original piece"   # reposts only
sourceUrl: "https://..."                        # reposts only
excerpt: "One or two sentences for listing pages."
tags: ["lowercase", "topical"]
```

A `sourceUrl` in frontmatter marks the article as a repost. No `sourceUrl` means it is an original.

## Template library

Seven post types. Pick the one that fits the material, and vary templates across consecutive posts so the feed does not settle into one shape; the sweep digest may suggest a template per candidate.

### 1. Share with comment (default repost)

Three parts:

1. **Intro.** One or two sentences: what the piece is and who wrote it.
2. **Excerpt.** A markdown blockquote carrying the source's main point in the source's own words. Verified quotes only, under about 60 words. One quote, or two short ones as separate blockquote paragraphs. End with an attribution line:

   ```
   > Quote text here.
   >
   > — Author/Speaker, via SourceName
   ```

3. **Outro.** Two or three sentences applying the piece to Iowa church planting.

### 2. Quick take

Two or three sentences total. No excerpt. For lighter shares where the link speaks for itself.

### 3. Story spotlight

Narrative-led. Retell the story compactly in our own words. No long quoting; at most one short verified quote inline. Close with one or two sentences on why it matters for Iowa.

### 4. By the numbers

Stat-led. Open with the number, then three to five short lines (or a compact list) unpacking it. Close with one Iowa sentence.

### 5. For your toolbox

Practical resource brief: what it is, who in Iowa it is for, and one concrete action step.

### 6. Iowa dispatch

An original on-the-ground update from Send Network Iowa. No `sourceUrl`. Free-form but short.

### 7. Roundup

Several links, each with a one-line comment, as a markdown list. Put each item's link inline in its line, since there is no single `sourceUrl`.

## Quoting rule

Verified quotes only, pulled verbatim from the source, wherever a quote appears in any template. Never invent, extend, or paraphrase inside quotation marks. Keep total quoted material under about 60 words per article, always attributed. These are third-party articles; we excerpt briefly and link, we do not republish.
